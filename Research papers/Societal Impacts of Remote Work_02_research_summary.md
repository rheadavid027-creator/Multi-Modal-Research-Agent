## Web Findings
The social impact of remote work is a topic of discussion among various organizations and researchers. According to Asynchronous Agile, remote work can have a positive social impact by extending inclusion programs beyond gender and race, reducing the environmental footprint of work, and allowing for decongestion of cities. Social Justice Ireland's national remote working strategy aims to make remote work a permanent feature of the Irish workplace, maximizing its benefits. The Ivey Business School blog discusses the impact of remote work on human connection and in-person networking within firms. Contour Interiors analyzes the social costs of remote working, including feelings of loneliness and disconnection. The Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada addresses the outcomes of remote work from home and employee mental well-being.

## Key Facts & Data Points
* ½ of all parents want to work remotely all the time (source: Asynchronous Agile)
* 9/10 of all women parents want to work remotely for at least three days each week (source: Asynchronous Agile)
* ⅔ of all people with disabilities want to work remotely all the time (source: Asynchronous Agile)
* Remote work can help with child care, particularly for women parents who often shoulder most of the parenting responsibilities (source: Asynchronous Agile)
* Remote work can have a positive social impact by extending inclusion programs beyond gender and race, reducing the environmental footprint of work, and allowing for decongestion of cities (source: Asynchronous Agile)
* Remote workers may often feel lonely or disconnected, leading to feelings of seclusion and damaging morale (source: Contour Interiors)
